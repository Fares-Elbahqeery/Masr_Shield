Core Evaluation Metrics

Standard accuracy alone is insufficient for fraud detection due to class imbalance and adversarial vulnerability. We track seven complementary metrics:

Accuracy

— %

Overall proportion of correct classifications across benign and scam classes.

Precision

— %

Ratio of true scam detections to all items flagged as scams (minimizing false alarms).

Recall (Sensitivity)

— %

Proportion of actual scam attempts successfully identified by the system.

F1-Score

—

Harmonic mean of precision and recall, balancing false alarms against missed attacks.

Consistency Rate

— %

Percentage of meaning-preserved rewrite pairs where classification remains identical.

False Positive Rate (FPR)

— %

Proportion of legitimate messages erroneously categorized as scam attempts.

False Negative Rate (FNR)

— %

Proportion of malicious scam messages that slip past detection undetected.

Experimental Methodology & Protocol Notes

Evaluation Protocol

All models will be evaluated using stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the development set followed by a single blind evaluation on the held-out test split. Random seeds must be documented to ensure full scientific reproducibility.

ConsistencyGuard Measurement Standard

The Consistency Rate is formally defined as the proportion of verified semantic pairs \((x_i, x_i')\) for which \(\hat{y}(x_i) = \hat{y}(x_i')\). Pairwise similarity is independently verified using Cosine Similarity on cross-lingual sentence embeddings (\(> 0.88\)) combined with native Egyptian Arabic speaker review.

Annotation & Labeling Guidelines

Ground-truth threat annotations follow a standardized multi-label taxonomy: Financial Fraud, Credential Harvesting, Authority Spoofing, and Benign Administrative Communication. Inter-annotator agreement will be quantified using Cohen's Kappa.

Research Limitations & Threats to Validity

  • Corpus Representativeness: Scammers continually adapt their phrasing. Static evaluation datasets may quickly experience distribution shift as new social engineering narratives emerge.
  • Dialectal Continuum Complexity: Arabic dialects are not discrete silos; Egyptian speakers fluidly blend code-switched English tokens, Franco-Arab (Arabizi), and Modern Standard Arabic within a single interaction.
  • Computational Resource Constraints: Training large foundational models with dense consistency regularization requires substantial GPU resources that may restrict real-time client-side deployment.
