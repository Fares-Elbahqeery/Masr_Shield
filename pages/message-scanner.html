Understanding Risk Indicators

Artificial Urgency & Panic Inducement
Scammers frequently demand immediate action (e.g., "Account suspended within 2 hours") to bypass rational scrutiny and trigger impulsive compliance.
Authority & Institutional Impersonation
Imitating banks, postal logistics (e.g., Egypt Post / البريد المصري), telecommunication providers, or governmental ministries to fabricate legitimacy.
Unsolicited Financial Windfalls or Job Offers
Promises of lottery winnings, unearned subsidies, or lucrative remote work requiring an upfront fee or registration deposit.
Dialectal Manipulation & Familiarity Cues
Using casual Egyptian Arabic colloquialisms (e.g., "يا باشا", "مبروك كسبت معانا") to disarm suspicion and establish false intimacy.

Warnings and Prototype Limitations

  • Assistive Heuristic Only: Detection signals are based on syntactic patterns and linguistic markers. They do not constitute forensic proof of cybercrime.
  • Potential False Positives & Negatives: Legitimate customer notifications may occasionally exhibit urgent language, while sophisticated spear-phishing messages may mimic formal communication flawlessly.
  • Active Research In Progress: This interface is a front-end research prototype. Actual automated detection models are not yet connected or validated.
